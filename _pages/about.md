Hi, I’m <span style="color:#0076df;">Mansi Dhamne</span>, a final-year Computer Engineering student at Sardar Patel Institute of Technology. My work is driven by a simple belief: AI should strengthen human well-being—and remain dependable when real-world conditions break ideal assumptions.

I’m currently a Research Intern at IIT Bombay, working with [Dr. Preeti Rao](https://www.ee.iitb.ac.in/web/people/preeti-rao/) on prosodic boundary prediction for child-directed speech systems, where I study how models trained on adult data fail when applied to children’s language. Previously, as a Research Fellow at Purdue University under [Dr. Matthew Ward](https://engineering.purdue.edu/BME/People/ptProfile?resource_id=42040), I designed Health Persona™, a multimodal monitoring platform for chronic illness management that integrates speech, text, and wearable signals into coherent longitudinal health narratives.

Across healthcare and education, my research focuses on a recurring challenge: models often perform well on clean benchmarks, yet behave unpredictably when inputs are ambiguous, noisy, or shaped by human behavior. I investigate these failures through diagnostic evaluation and interpretability, asking not just whether a system works, but when, why, and how it breaks. My work has been published at EMNLP 2025 and accepted to AAAI 2026 workshops, with an emphasis on robust and interpretable multimodal learning for high-stakes settings.

Broadly, I am interested in:
- Multimodal systems that capture the depth and variability of human behavior across contexts
- Interpretability and evaluation methods that expose brittle reasoning before deployment failures occur

In addition to research, I’ve worked as a Software Development Engineer Intern at early-stage startups, building production systems at the intersection of ML and full-stack engineering. My work has been recognized through honors including the IBM Maitreyee 2025 Research Showcase Award.

I’m motivated by problems where technical rigor meets real-world uncertainty—and where earning human trust matters as much as performance.
